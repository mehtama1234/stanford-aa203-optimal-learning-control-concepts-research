Hamiltonian for Optimal Control

A combined expression that packages immediate cost with how actions move the state into future cost.

Ordinary Problem

An action must be judged both by what it costs now and by how it changes the state that future costs depend on.

Naive Approach

Choose the action with the smallest immediate cost.

Why it fails: The cheap action now can steer the state into an expensive region.

Mathematical Object

The Hamiltonian in an optimal-control necessary condition.

Operation

Combine stage cost, dynamics, and costate sensitivity, then choose controls that satisfy the necessary condition.

Worked Example

A rocket burn costs fuel immediately but may reduce future position error; the Hamiltonian puts both effects in one local calculation.

Boundary And Failure

Assumption Boundary

It gives necessary conditions under model and smoothness assumptions, not automatic global optimality.

Failure Mode

A trajectory satisfies local equations but is not the best or violates practical constraints.

Recognize It

Use it when a local action must be priced by immediate and future state effects together.
