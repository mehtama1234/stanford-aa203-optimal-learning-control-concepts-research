Collocation

A direct method that enforces dynamics at selected points along a discretized trajectory.

01 · the ordinary pressure

Why this idea has to exist

The optimizer needs a finite way to ensure the path obeys continuous dynamics.

A common wrong first move is: Check the dynamics only at the endpoints. It fails when a path can look valid at endpoints while bending through impossible motion between them.

02 · the object

What the math keeps track of

The working object is collocation points and defect constraints. Use it when a continuous trajectory is represented by pieces and dynamics are enforced at sample points.

What the object does: Force the derivative implied by the path representation to match the dynamics at chosen points.

03 · read it with your hands

What to inspect first

Before naming the method, point to collocation points and defect constraints in the setup. Ask what a person could measure, command, update, price, or forbid in one small run.

Now apply one command or one update. Force the derivative implied by the path representation to match the dynamics at chosen points. After that operation, ask what changed in the next state, the path, the value, the constraint set, the policy, or the learned model. If nothing concrete changes, the explanation is still only a label.

The world check is the example: A drone path spline is checked at interior points so acceleration and velocity stay physically consistent. Try the same question on another car, drone, rover, robot arm, or reward signal. The concept should still name what changes, what is paid for, and what can fail.

04 · one concrete run

Work it through before naming the formula

A robot arm moving around a fixture should not be checked only at the start and end. Collocation picks interior points, makes state and action variables there, and forces the dynamics to hold at those points so the path cannot quietly cheat between them.

the actual math, one level deeper

Collocation uses polynomial or grid approximations and enforces defect equations at selected points. Smaller defects mean the discretized path better matches continuous dynamics.

05 · boundary

Where the idea stops working

The polynomial or grid representation must resolve the motion between points.

When that condition fails, look for this visible break: too few collocation points let the optimizer exploit gaps between checks.

Recognize it in a new problem: Use it when a continuous trajectory is represented by pieces and dynamics are enforced at sample points.
