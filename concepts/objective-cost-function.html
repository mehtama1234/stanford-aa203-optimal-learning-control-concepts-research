Objective / Cost Function

The scoring rule that says which futures are better or worse.

01 · the ordinary pressure

Why this idea has to exist

A controller needs a way to compare a fast route, a smooth route, a safe route, and a cheap route.

The tempting shortcut is simple: Optimize the most visible target, such as time or distance. But that shortcut breaks because the visible target can hide fuel, safety, wear, comfort, or constraint risk.

02 · the object

What the math keeps track of

The controller keeps track of a stage cost, terminal cost, reward, or objective functional. This answers a practical question: ask what future mistake the scoring rule would punish.

The operation is concrete: Accumulate cost across the trajectory and choose the action sequence with the best total.

03 · one concrete run

Work it through before naming the formula

A car that reaches a parking spot fastest may scrape the wall. A car that never moves is safe but useless. The cost is the scoreboard that says which future is better: time, distance from the goal, steering effort, comfort, and collision risk all get counted in one place.

the actual math, one level deeper

A typical objective adds stage costs along the path and a terminal cost at the end. The weights are not decoration; they state the tradeoff the controller will actually obey.

04 · boundary

Where the idea stops working

The written cost is only as good as the values and tradeoffs it encodes.

If that boundary is crossed, the visible failure is this: the controller optimizes a proxy and produces behavior the designer did not intend.

Recognize it in a new problem: Ask what future mistake the scoring rule would punish.
