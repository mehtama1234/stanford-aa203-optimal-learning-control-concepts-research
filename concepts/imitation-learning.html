Imitation Learning

Learn a policy from demonstrations of someone or something already doing the task.

01 · the ordinary pressure

Why this idea has to exist

Sometimes it is easier to show good behavior than to write the reward or model that would produce it.

The tempting shortcut is simple: Copy the demonstrated action for every observed state. But that shortcut breaks because small mistakes move the learner into states the demonstrator never showed.

02 · the object

What the math keeps track of

The controller keeps track of a dataset of state-action demonstrations and a learned policy. This answers a practical question: use it when examples of good behavior are easier to obtain than a reliable reward or dynamics model.

The operation is concrete: Fit a policy that maps observations or states to demonstrated actions.

03 · one concrete run

Work it through before naming the formula

A person can show a robot how to pull a drawer without writing a reward for every contact force and handle angle. Imitation learning turns demonstrations into an action rule. The danger is closed-loop drift: one small mistake puts the robot in a state the teacher never showed.

the actual math, one level deeper

The data are state-action pairs from an expert. The learned policy maps observations to actions. Training loss measures action mismatch on the demonstrated states, not on every state the learned policy may later visit.

04 · boundary

Where the idea stops working

The demonstration distribution must cover states the learned policy will visit.

If that boundary is crossed, the visible failure is this: the learned policy drifts off-demo and has no training signal for recovery.

Recognize it in a new problem: Use it when examples of good behavior are easier to obtain than a reliable reward or dynamics model.
