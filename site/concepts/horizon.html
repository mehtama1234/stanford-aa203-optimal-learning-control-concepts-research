Horizon

How far into the future the controller is asked to reason.

Ordinary Problem

Planning one second ahead and planning the whole mission are different problems.

Naive Approach

Pick an arbitrary lookahead window.

Why it fails: A short horizon can miss delayed consequences; a long horizon can be too expensive or depend on unreliable forecasts.

Mathematical Object

A finite horizon, infinite horizon, or receding horizon.

Operation

Limit or extend the future cost summed by the optimization.

Worked Example

A car changing lanes must look far enough ahead to avoid a merge conflict, not just avoid the next meter.

Boundary And Failure

Assumption Boundary

The horizon should match the time scale at which consequences become visible.

Failure Mode

The controller repeatedly makes greedy moves because the real cost lies just beyond the horizon.

Recognize It

Ask when the consequences of a bad action first appear.
