Behavioral Cloning

The simplest imitation-learning setup: treat expert actions as labels and train a policy by supervised learning.

01 · the ordinary pressure

Why this idea has to exist

The learner has examples of what action the expert took in each state and wants to mimic that mapping.

The tempting shortcut is simple: Assume high supervised accuracy means good closed-loop control. But that shortcut breaks because a small prediction error changes the next state, and errors accumulate because future inputs are policy-generated.

02 · the object

What the math keeps track of

The controller keeps track of a supervised policy model trained on expert state-action pairs. This answers a practical question: look for imitation framed as ordinary supervised prediction of expert actions.

The operation is concrete: Minimize prediction error between policy actions and expert actions.

03 · one concrete run

Work it through before naming the formula

A lane-following policy trained on expert driving images may fail after drifting slightly toward the lane edge. the actual math, one level deeper

The mathematical object is a supervised policy model trained on expert state-action pairs. The operation is: Minimize prediction error between policy actions and expert actions. The boundary is: The training data must represent the states induced by the learned policy.

04 · boundary

Where the idea stops working

The training data must represent the states induced by the learned policy.

If that boundary is crossed, the visible failure is this: compounding errors move the policy outside the expert dataset.
