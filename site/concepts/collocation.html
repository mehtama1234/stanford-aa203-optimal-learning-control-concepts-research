Collocation

A direct method that enforces dynamics at selected points along a discretized trajectory.

01 · the ordinary pressure

Why this idea has to exist

The optimizer needs a finite way to ensure the path obeys continuous dynamics.

The tempting shortcut is simple: Check the dynamics only at the endpoints. But that shortcut breaks because a path can look valid at endpoints while bending through impossible motion between them.

02 · the object

What the math keeps track of

The controller keeps track of collocation points and defect constraints. This answers a practical question: use it when a continuous trajectory is represented by pieces and dynamics are enforced at sample points.

The operation is concrete: Force the derivative implied by the path representation to match the dynamics at chosen points.

03 · one concrete run

Work it through before naming the formula

A robot arm moving around a fixture should not be checked only at the start and end. Collocation picks interior points, makes state and action variables there, and forces the dynamics to hold at those points so the path cannot quietly cheat between them.

the actual math, one level deeper

Collocation uses polynomial or grid approximations and enforces defect equations at selected points. Smaller defects mean the discretized path better matches continuous dynamics.

04 · boundary

Where the idea stops working

The polynomial or grid representation must resolve the motion between points.

If that boundary is crossed, the visible failure is this: too few collocation points let the optimizer exploit gaps between checks.

Recognize it in a new problem: Use it when a continuous trajectory is represented by pieces and dynamics are enforced at sample points.
