# Research Seed

AA203 should be treated as one course-length argument about how to choose actions over time when actions have consequences, constraints, uncertainty, and computational cost.

## First Course Spine

1. Define the control problem: state, action, dynamics, objective, constraints, horizon.
2. Learn the optimization language: gradients, constraints, convexity, necessary conditions.
3. Move from static optimization to paths: calculus of variations and indirect methods.
4. Turn paths into computation: direct transcription, shooting, collocation, and numerical optimization.
5. Use value as the organizing object: dynamic programming and stochastic dynamic programming.
6. Exploit local structure: LQR-style methods and quadratic approximations.
7. Keep decisions feasible while replanning: MPC and reachability.
8. Learn from data or experts when models are incomplete: imitation learning, RL, policy optimization, value methods, and model-based RL.

Each page should explain the pressure that forces the method: why one-shot optimization is not enough, why time coupling matters, why uncertainty changes the problem, why feasibility cannot be checked only at the end, and why learning is introduced after model-based control rather than as a replacement for it.
