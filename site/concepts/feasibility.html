Feasibility

Whether there exists any action sequence that satisfies the constraints from the current state.

01 · the ordinary pressure

Why this idea has to exist

A controller cannot optimize a plan that no physical or legal action can execute.

The tempting shortcut is simple: Solve for the best plan and check constraints afterward. But that shortcut breaks because if no admissible plan exists, the optimizer's output is not a plan; it is evidence that the setup is impossible or too tight.

02 · the object

What the math keeps track of

The controller keeps track of a feasible set or recursively feasible set. This answers a practical question: before asking what is optimal, ask whether anything legal remains possible.

The operation is concrete: Check whether at least one trajectory obeys all dynamics and constraints.

03 · read it with your hands

What to inspect first

Point to the physical record or mathematical object before naming the method. In this concept, that object is a feasible set or recursively feasible set. Ask what a person could measure, command, update, price, or forbid in one small run.

Now apply one command or one update. Check whether at least one trajectory obeys all dynamics and constraints. After that operation, ask what changed in the next state, the path, the value, the constraint set, the policy, or the learned model. If nothing concrete changes, the explanation is still only a label.

The world check is the example: If a car is too close to a wall at high speed, no braking command may avoid impact within the remaining distance. A reader should be able to replace that setup with another car, drone, rover, robot arm, or reward signal and keep the same control question alive.

04 · one concrete run

Work it through before naming the formula

A car boxed between two trucks may have no legal lane change, no legal stop, and no legal acceleration that avoids trouble. Feasibility asks this before arguing about best behavior: is there at least one legal future left?

the actual math, one level deeper

The feasible set contains states and actions satisfying dynamics, bounds, and path constraints. Optimization starts only after this set is nonempty.

05 · boundary

Where the idea stops working

Feasibility depends on the current state, constraint model, horizon, and actuator limits.

If that boundary is crossed, the visible failure is this: an mpc controller repeatedly solves impossible subproblems and returns unstable or emergency behavior.

Recognize it in a new problem: Before asking what is optimal, ask whether anything legal remains possible.
