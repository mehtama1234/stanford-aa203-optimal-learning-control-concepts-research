Distribution Shift in Imitation

The gap between states in the expert dataset and states the learned policy actually visits.

01 · the ordinary pressure

Why this idea has to exist

A policy's own mistakes change the inputs it sees next.

The tempting shortcut is simple: Evaluate the policy only on held-out expert states. But that shortcut breaks because closed-loop execution creates new states that were rare or absent in the demonstrations.

02 · the object

What the math keeps track of

The controller keeps track of a state distribution induced by a policy. This answers a practical question: ask whether the learner will see the same state distribution as the expert data.

The operation is concrete: Compare or correct the mismatch between expert data and learner rollouts.

03 · one concrete run

Work it through before naming the formula

A cloned driving policy may drift a little right of lane center. The expert data has few examples from that off-center state because the expert rarely made the mistake. The learned policy now has to act in a part of the world where it was not trained.

the actual math, one level deeper

Training data comes from the expert's state distribution, but deployment states come from the learned policy's distribution. Small action errors can compound because those distributions differ.

04 · boundary

Where the idea stops working

The issue matters most when actions affect future observations.

If that boundary is crossed, the visible failure is this: errors compound until the policy reaches states where it has no competent behavior.

Recognize it in a new problem: Ask whether the learner will see the same state distribution as the expert data.
